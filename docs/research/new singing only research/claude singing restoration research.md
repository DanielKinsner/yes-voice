# Modern Vocal Restoration & Polish for SINGING: A Technical & Licensing Field Guide for the YES Audio Suite

## TL;DR
- **The single most important finding:** No commercially-shippable, singing-tuned, all-in-one restoration model exists yet — your edge is a HYBRID architecture: route audio through MossFormer2 (Apache 2.0, already integrated) and Mel-Band RoFormer-class separation, then polish with classic, deterministic DSP (AR-interpolation declick, WPE dereverb, multiband de-ess, spectral-subtraction/Wiener denoise) that YOU control and that is patent-free and license-clean. Treat every off-the-shelf speech model as a denoiser that will flatten vibrato, smear formants, and produce "musical noise" on sustained vowels unless gated and dialed back.
- **Licensing reality:** Apache-2.0 (MossFormer/ClearerVoice), MIT (Demucs code+weights, Spleeter, AudioSR, DeepFilterNet dual MIT/Apache, nara-wpe, lucidrains RoFormer code) are commercially shippable in a one-time-purchase desktop app. The DANGER ZONE is pretrained *weights*: the popular Mel-Band RoFormer vocal checkpoints (Kim, viperx) have shifting/ambiguous licenses (unlicensed→GPL-3.0→MIT), BSRNN and FRCRN carry non-commercial (CC BY-NC-SA) signals, and Demucs weights are MIT but separating copyrighted songs is a *content* rights issue separate from model license. Pedalboard (Spotify) is GPL-3.0 — avoid linking it into a closed-source product.
- **Singing ≠ speech:** Vibrato is a *desired* periodic modulation of the fundamental at a 5–7 Hz rate with an extent of about 6–8% (roughly ±1 semitone), per the Vocal Vibrato review in the Journal of Voice (PMC8282806); sustained harmonic tones, wide dynamics, expressive breaths, and formant-defined timbre mean speech-tuned VAD/denoise/de-ess/de-breath tools make actively wrong assumptions. Build singing-aware processing: vibrato-preserving noise gating, formant-aware (source-filter/LPC/cepstral) protection, and breath as a controllable element, not noise to nuke.

## Key Findings

### 1. The fundamental speech-vs-singing distinction (this must drive every design choice)
Speech-restoration tools embed assumptions that are *correct for talking and wrong for singing*:
- **Voice Activity Detection (VAD):** Speech denoisers exploit inter-word/inter-sentence pauses to learn the noise profile ("silent intervals" — the basis of many DNN denoisers, e.g., the Sounds-of-Silence approach). Singers sustain pitched tones for seconds with no pauses; a VAD-driven noise estimator either finds no silence or mistakes a held note's decay for noise.
- **Vibrato:** A sung note's fundamental is frequency-modulated — a *desired* periodic modulation at a 5–7 Hz rate with an extent of ~6–8% (about ±1 semitone), per the Journal of Voice vibrato review (PMC8282806); Western classical vibrato runs ~4.5–6.5 Hz with ~50–120 cents peak-to-peak extent. Crucially, pop/rock vibrato can sit at the *high* end: an acoustic analysis of 240 sustained notes across 21 a-cappella Freddie Mercury recordings (Herbst et al., *Logoped Phoniatr Vocol* 2017, PubMed 27079680) found "a surprisingly high mean fundamental frequency modulation rate (vibrato) of 7.0 Hz, reaching the range of vocal tremor" — i.e., real sung vibrato can land exactly where a naïve spectral gate or "pitch stabilizer" would read it as jitter/noise and flatten it. Pitch-shift restoration research confirms the broader risk: classical signal-processing approaches "exhibit well-known trade-offs such as formant shifts and robotic coloration" on singing (Liu & Akama, arXiv 2601.10345, 2026).
- **Formants:** Vocal timbre/identity lives in formant structure (resonances of the vocal tract). Generative/vocoder-based restoration (VoiceFixer, Miipher, AudioSR) resynthesizes audio and can shift formants — a "chipmunk"/over-processed effect. Sonarworks documents that AI voice processing produces formant-shifting errors and metallic/digital coloration.
- **Breath:** In speech post, breath is reduced/removed (Waves DeBreath, RX Breath Control are speech-tuned). In singing, breath is expressive — your tool must make breath *controllable* (attenuate by a user amount), never gate it to zero.
- **Sibilance:** A singer sibilates at different frequencies depending on dynamics (higher when singing softly, lower/harder when belting), so static speech de-ess bands misfire on sung vocals.
- **Musical noise:** Aggressive spectral subtraction yields the "watery"/"chirpy"/"space-monkey" artifact (iZotope's own terminology for over-denoising). On sustained vowels with rich harmonics this is far more audible than on transient speech.

**Design implication:** wherever you reuse a speech model, (a) disable or replace its VAD-based noise estimation with a user-painted noise profile or a continuous estimator (IMCRA), (b) protect harmonic partials and their vibrato sidebands (model vibrato up to ~7 Hz as *signal*), (c) prefer masking/subtractive approaches you can dial back over generative resynthesis when fidelity to the original performance matters, and (d) expose breath as a separate, scalable control.

### 2. Industry-standard tools — what they are, speech vs. singing, shippability

These are **competitors/benchmarks**, not components you can embed (all proprietary/closed). They define the quality bar.

- **iZotope RX (current version RX 12; RX 11 was the prior gen):** The post-production industry standard, winner of technical Academy/Emmy awards. Modules: Spectral De-noise, Voice De-noise, Dialogue Isolate (now merged-in Dialogue De-reverb, real-time + new highest-quality offline mode + multiband in Advanced), Music Rebalance (ML stem separation — historically used Spleeter, now "the most modern neural network available"), De-click, De-crackle, De-hum (static + dynamic adaptive, up to 1024 notch filters), De-ess, De-plosive, Mouth De-click, Breath Control, Spectral Repair, Dialogue Contour, Spectral Recovery (bandwidth extension). **Most modules are speech/dialogue-tuned**, but Music Rebalance and the general spectral tools (De-click, De-hum, Spectral Repair, Spectral De-noise) work on singing. ### 3. Open-source / research models — capabilities and commercial-shippability

**SAFE TO SHIP (permissive code + permissive weights):**
- **MossFormer / MossFormer2 (Alibaba/Tongyi SpeechLab, ClearerVoice-Studio):** Apache-2.0 (confirmed on both repo and the HuggingFace model weights, e.g., `alibabasglab/MossFormer2_SE_48K` license: apache-2.0). Models: MossFormer2_SE_48K (48 kHz speech enhancement), MossFormerGAN_SE_16K, FRCRN_SE_16K, MossFormer2_SS_16K (separation), MossFormer2_SR_48K (super-resolution, MossFormer2+HiFi-GAN). **CRITICAL: these are SPEECH models** — SE_48K is "trained on a clean speech dataset and designed to work with clean speech inputs"; separation is speaker-vs-speaker, not vocal-vs-instrument. They will denoise a sung vocal but with speech assumptions. Architecture: time-domain encoder-separator-decoder; MossFormer (gated single-head attention + convolution) + FSMN-based "RNN-free" recurrent module. **You already have this; use SE_48K as one denoise option, clearly flagged as speech-tuned, with conservative defaults.**
- **Demucs / HTDemucs / HTDemucs_6s (Meta):** MIT — *both code and pretrained weights* are MIT (confirmed: Meta's repo, HF mirrors). Hybrid Transformer Demucs, 44.1 kHz stereo; the facebookresearch/demucs README reports **9.00 dB SDR on the MUSDB HQ test set, and 9.20 dB with sparse attention kernels + per-source fine-tuning** (trained on MUSDB18-HQ + 800 extra songs); 4-stem (vocals/drums/bass/other) and 6-stem (+guitar/piano). Exportable to ONNX (Mixxx GSoC 2025 produced a working ONNX/ORT C++ pipeline). **This is your workhorse vocal-isolation/stem engine, fully commercially shippable.** Caveat: separating someone else's copyrighted song to release derivative stems is a *content licensing* issue independent of the MIT model license.
- **Spleeter (Deezer):** MIT (code + pretrained models). Fast (100× real-time GPU), 2/4/5-stem. Older/lower quality than Demucs/RoFormer; the model that originally powered RX Music Rebalance and SpectraLayers Unmix. Shippable.
- **Open-Unmix (UMX):** MIT, open-source reference separation model. Shippable but superseded in quality.
- **AudioSR (haoheliu/versatile_audio_super_resolution):** MIT (confirmed via PyPI `audiosr` and repo). Diffusion-based super-resolution/bandwidth extension; upsamples 2–16 kHz input to 48 kHz, **works on music, speech, and sound effects** (more general than speech-only SR). Caveat: trained on specific cutoff patterns; "struggles when encountering unfamiliar cutoff patterns" like MP3 loss — pre-low-pass recommended. Generative → can alter detail; A/B against original.
- **nara-wpe (Paderborn):** MIT. Reference Python implementation of Weighted Prediction Error dereverberation (offline iterative, block-online, frame-online). Algorithm is the blind multi-channel/single-channel long-term linear-prediction method (Yoshioka & Nakatani 2012). **Reimplement the math in Rust** (it's deterministic linear algebra) for a license-clean, singing-safe de-reverb stage.
- **DeepFilterNet / DeepFilterNet2 / DeepFilterNet3:** Dual-licensed **MIT OR Apache-2.0** (code + pretrained weights). Full-band 48 kHz, real-time (RTF 0.04–0.19 on a laptop CPU), **already implemented in Rust** (libDF; uses `tract` for inference; community `deepfilter-rt` runs it via ONNX Runtime). Two-stage: ERB-domain gain stage + deep-filter complex stage; SNR-gated. **Speech-tuned and only enhances periodicity up to ~5 kHz** — will work on a sung vocal's noise but is built around speech harmonic assumptions; good as a fast "light denoise" option, flagged as such.

**RESEARCH/REFERENCE ONLY or LICENSE-RISKY (do not ship weights without verification):**
- **BS-RoFormer / Mel-Band RoFormer (ByteDance; Lu, Wang, Won, arXiv 2310.01809):** **SOTA music source separation**, especially vocals. On MUSDB18HQ, Mel-RoFormer beats BS-RoFormer on vocals (**11.21 dB vs 10.78 dB, +0.43**) and drums (9.91 vs 9.61 dB) but lags on bass; the paper reports "an average gain of 0.5 dB across all scenarios" from Mel-band projection. BS-RoFormer trained on MUSDB18HQ + 500 extra songs reached **11.99 dB average SDR and ranked first in the Music Separation track of the Sound Demixing Challenge 2023 (SDX23)**. lucidrains' *code* is MIT. **The danger is the WEIGHTS.** The popular `KimberleyJSN/melbandroformer` "Kim Vocal" checkpoint was **unlicensed → GPL-3.0 (June 2025) → MIT (current tag)**, and downstream mirrors (Intel OpenVINO, ComfyUI ports) still carry GPL-3.0 — you must confirm the exact HF commit/LICENSE before shipping. ZFTurbo's Music-Source-Separation-Training framework is MIT (LICENSE committed 2024-11-04, ~1 year after the v1.0.0 release that had no license), and its release checkpoints inherit MIT — but the widely-used **viperx** checkpoints lack an explicit standalone license; treat as unspecified and verify. **Recommendation: train or commission your own Mel-Band RoFormer vocal weights on a license-clean dataset (e.g., MUSDB18-HQ + cleared data) so you own the checkpoint outright.**
- **BSRNN (Band-Split RNN, Luo & Yu, ByteDance, arXiv 2209.15174):** **No official open weights/code from authors.** Unofficial reproductions only; the paper carries CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial) signals. **Use as architecture inspiration only.**
- **FRCRN (Alibaba, in ClearerVoice-Studio):** Bundled in the Apache repo, but the paper assets show CC BY-NC-SA, and the model-card license is unconfirmed — **verify the actual LICENSE/model card before commercial use.** Also 16 kHz speech-only → unsuitable for full-band sung vocals.
- **VoiceFixer (Liu et al.) / VoiceFixer2:** VoiceFixer2 is **MIT**. General speech restoration (noise + reverb + clipping + low-res in one model), ResUNet analysis + neural vocoder synthesis, 44.1 kHz. **Speech-only and generative (~122M params)** — it resynthesizes via vocoder and *will* alter sung timbre/formants. Reference architecture; risky on singing fidelity.
- **Miipher / Miipher-2 (Google):** **Closed-source, code/weights NOT released** ("due to potential misuse risks"). Parametric resynthesis (USM/w2v-BERT features + WaveFit/HiFi-GAN vocoder). Reference only. **ReverbMiipher** (extends Miipher-2 to *preserve and control* reverberation rather than strip it) is an important design idea for singing — reverb as a controllable feature — but also unreleased.
- **Sidon (open Miipher alternative):** Open-source parametric speech restoration, 102 languages, 48 kHz, uses open w2v-BERT 2.0 + HiFi-GAN; competitive with Miipher. Speech-focused; check repo license. Reference for a generative-restoration tier.
- **Resemble Enhance:** Open speech enhancement+restoration (denoise + generative restore). Speech-tuned; check license (Resemble AI). Reference.
- **FullSubNet / FlowAVSE / NU-Wave / FlashSR / UniverSR:** Various SE/SR research models; NU-Wave/AudioSR/FlashSR/UniverSR are bandwidth-extension; most are research-licensed — verify individually.
- **Diffusion/generative restoration (universal speech enhancement via score-based diffusion, DiffSinger-style mel denoisers, "Self-supervised restoration of singing voice degraded by pitch shifting using shallow diffusion" arXiv 2601.10345):** The singing-specific paper (Liu & Akama, submitted Jan 2026) is the rare one that explicitly targets **sung** voice and **preserves melody/timing**: mel-space shallow diffusion (1-D temporal U-Net), conditioned on CREPE F0 (65–800 Hz) + volume envelope + ContentVec, NSF-HiFiGAN vocoder, self-supervised via WORLD pitch-shift pairs; it reports best FAD (7.886 vs 19–78 for baselines) and F0 RMSE 2.881 cents. This is your best architectural template for a *future* singing-native restoration model. Research code; not a shippable product.

**Classic DSP libraries:**
- **noisereduce (Tim Sainburg):** spectral gating (the algorithm behind much "spectral gate" denoise); permissive (MIT). Singing-friendlier than speech denoisers because it's a general spectral gate, but still needs careful thresholds to avoid musical noise on sustained vowels.
- **librosa** (ISC), **torchaudio** (BSD-2) — analysis/feature tooling; permissive.
- **pedalboard (Spotify):** **GPL-3.0** — *do not statically/dynamically link into a closed-source one-time-purchase app.* Use only if you GPL your product (you won't).
- **CREPE (MIT), pYIN (in librosa), SWIPE, WORLD vocoder (modified-BSD), Harvest/DIO:** pitch trackers and vocoder analysis for vibrato-aware/formant-aware processing — permissive, embeddable.

### 4. Algorithm/DSP theory you'll implement (the deterministic, license-clean core)

- **STFT/spectrogram, phase, complex masking:** All spectral processing rides on STFT/iSTFT. For mask-based denoise/separation, prefer **complex-domain masks (cIRM)** or deep filtering over magnitude-only masks to preserve phase and reduce musical noise. Griffin-Lim phase reconstruction is a fallback only when phase is lost (avoid for high-fidelity vocal work; it smears transients).
- **Denoise math:** Spectral subtraction (Boll) → musical noise; Wiener filtering (MMSE in the mean-square sense); **MMSE-STSA and log-MMSE (Ephraim-Malah)** reduce musical noise; **IMCRA** for noise-PSD estimation *without* requiring speech pauses — important for singing because it tracks noise continuously rather than relying on VAD/silence. This is the key swap that makes a "speech" denoiser singing-safe.
- **Declick — AR interpolation (Janssen; Godsill & Rayner):** Detect clicks via AR-model prediction error; reconstruct missing samples by **least-squares autoregressive interpolation** (Janssen et al.), with the Bayesian/MCMC extensions in Godsill & Rayner's *Digital Audio Restoration* (1998). For music with strong harmonics, the **sinusoid + AR model** handles tonal content better (gburlet/audio-restore implements this from the book). Deterministic, patent-free, ideal for a Rust/C++ module. Use for mouth clicks, lip smacks, digital clicks, vinyl crackle.
- **WPE dereverberation math:** Blind, based on long-term linear prediction — predict the late-reverberation tail from delayed past STFT frames and subtract it, iteratively reweighting by the estimated desired-signal PSD (Yoshioka & Nakatani 2012; nara-wpe). Single- and multi-channel; offline iterative version is best for render-and-export. **Especially delicate for singing** — over-suppression kills the natural resonance/sustain that makes a vocal sound alive, so expose a wet/dry and tail-length control and default conservative.
- **De-essing:** Sibilance sits ~5–10 kHz (broader 2–12 kHz; worst esses ~5–8 kHz). Two architectures: **wide-band** (a frequency-dependent compressor that ducks the whole signal when sibilance crosses a threshold) vs. **split-band** (attenuate only the sibilant band). Detection is via an EQ'd/filtered sidechain so the sibilant frequencies are most prominent. For singing, make the detection band *dynamic*/program-dependent (sibilance frequency shifts with dynamics) and prefer split-band to avoid pumping the whole vocal.
- **HPSS, sinusoidal modeling, harmonic+noise models:** Harmonic-percussive source separation and harmonic+noise (HNM) decomposition let you treat the *harmonic* (pitched, vibrato-bearing) part separately from the *noise/breath* part — the right primitive for "reduce breath without touching sustained vowels" and "denoise the noise floor while protecting harmonic partials."
- **Source-filter model / LPC / cepstrum / formant tracking:** Model the voice as glottal source × vocal-tract filter; track formants via LPC or cepstral liftering and **protect/preserve them** during any spectral surgery or resynthesis to keep vocal character. This is the theoretical guardrail against "chipmunk"/metallic artifacts.
- **Vibrato in spectrograms:** Appears as periodic ~5–7 Hz lateral wiggle of each harmonic (frequency-modulation sidebands; can reach ~7 Hz in pop/rock). Any process that treats narrow time-frequency fluctuation as noise/instability (some spectral gates, some pitch "stabilizers") will flatten it. Use longer analysis windows / harmonic tracking so vibrato is modeled as signal, not error.
- **Audio inpainting / spectral repair:** For surgical fixes (a cough, a chair squeak over a held note), reconstruct the masked time-frequency region from surrounding context — classically via AR/sinusoidal extrapolation (RX Interpolate/Spectral Repair), increasingly via learned inpainting. Deterministic AR/sinusoidal methods are shippable today; learned inpainting is a future tier.

### 5. Order of operations (signal chain) — adapted for singing
iZotope's canonical "deepest damage first" order (from their official Order of Operations guide): **(1) De-clip → (2) stereo/azimuth fixes → (3) clicks/crackle (De-click, Mouth De-click, Interpolate) → (4) hum (De-hum) → (5) broadband noise, steady then variable (Spectral/Voice De-noise) → (6) variable noise: de-ess, breath, plosive, de-reverb last.** The rationale: clipping and clicks corrupt the signal that later stages must analyze, so remove them first ("mud flows downstream"). The same guide cautions to de-noise only "to a place where you notice significant artifacts, and then back off the reduction slider until you don't hear the artifacts anymore."

For a **singing** pipeline, adapt as:
1. **De-clip** (reconstruct clipped peaks; leave headroom).
2. **De-hum / De-buzz** (notch 50/60 Hz + harmonics; surgical, doesn't touch the vocal band much).
3. **Declick / Decrackle / Mouth de-click / De-plosive** (AR-interpolation; transient, surgical).
4. **(Optional) Stem separation** (Demucs/Mel-Band RoFormer) if you must isolate the vocal from a mix first — note this introduces its own artifacts, so do it early and treat the *vocal stem* downstream.
5. **Dereverb (WPE)** — debated whether before or after denoise. Pros of dereverb-after-denoise: denoiser sees uniform noise, not reverb-modulated noise. Pros of denoise-after-dereverb: cleaner reverb-tail estimation. Practitioner consensus (Gearspace, with an iZotope dev chiming in): try both, judge by ear; EQ out room nodes first so neither has to work as hard. **For singing, keep dereverb gentle to preserve sustain.**
6. **Broadband denoise** (IMCRA-estimated Wiener/log-MMSE or MossFormer2_SE_48K, conservative) — back off until "space monkeys" disappear, then back off more.
7. **De-ess** (split-band, dynamic band).
8. **Breath control** (attenuate by user amount — never gate to zero for singing).
9. **(Optional) Super-resolution / spectral recovery** (AudioSR) to restore air/HF if the source is band-limited — A/B against original, generative risk.
10. **Spectral repair** for any remaining surgical spots.

### 6. Practical Rust/Tauri architecture for the offline app

- **Inference runtime:** Use the **`ort` crate** (pyke) — a safe Rust wrapper over Microsoft's ONNX Runtime, production-proven (Google Magika, SurrealDB, Supabase edge functions), reported at 3–5× faster than Python with 60–80% less memory, supporting CUDA/TensorRT/CoreML/OpenVINO/DirectML execution providers. This is the right default for offline batch inference of MossFormer2, Demucs, RoFormer, DeepFilterNet, AudioSR. Pattern: build one `Session` at startup, wrap in `Arc`, reuse across threads (`Session` is `Send`+`Sync`). Use `GraphOptimizationLevel::Level3` and the `compiler`/`ModelCompiler` for ahead-of-time EP graphs to cut cold-start.
- **Alternatives:** **`candle`** (HuggingFace, pure-Rust, no C++ dependency — good for custom architectures/smaller binaries, and DeepFilterNet-style models), **`tch-rs`** (libtorch bindings — full PyTorch ecosystem, heavier, good if you train/fine-tune), **`tract`** (pure-Rust, what DeepFilterNet itself uses for real-time). For an offline desktop app shipping multiple large models, **`ort` + ONNX is the pragmatic choice**; reserve `candle`/`tract` for models that export poorly to ONNX or where you want zero native deps.
- **Model export pipeline:** PyTorch → ONNX via `torch.onnx.export` / HF Optimum. Note STFT/iSTFT ops sometimes don't export cleanly (the Mixxx GSoC Demucs→ONNX project specifically stripped STFT/iSTFT to "outer" ops and did them in host code) — plan to do STFT/iSTFT in Rust (rustfft) and feed spectra to the ONNX graph, or validate numerically after export.
- **Deterministic DSP in Rust:** `rustfft`/`realfft` (FFT), `fundsp` (DSP graph/filters), `rubato` (resampling), `symphonia` (decode), `hound`/`creak` (WAV I/O), `dasp` (sample/frame primitives). Implement spectral subtraction/Wiener/log-MMSE, IMCRA, AR-interpolation declick, WPE, multiband de-ess, de-hum notch banks here — all license-clean and fully under your control. JUCE/C++ is justified if you want to reuse mature DSP or ship VST/AU plugin versions alongside the standalone; otherwise pure Rust keeps the stack unified.
- **Hybrid pipeline pattern:** ML stage (separation/denoise via ONNX) → deterministic DSP polish (declick/dehum/deess/dereverb/breath in Rust) → optional generative SR. Run ML on GPU when available, fall back to CPU; chunk long files with overlap-add to bound memory; expose every stage as toggle + intensity so users (and your defaults) can keep processing conservative for singing.
- **Threading/UX:** Tauri (Rust backend + TS/web frontend) — do all DSP/inference on a Rust worker thread pool, stream progress to the webview; never block the UI thread. Offline/non-real-time means you can use large FFTs, multiple passes, and high-quality (slow) settings impossible in real-time tools — make that your quality differentiator.

## Caveats
- **Model license ≠ content license.** Demucs/Spleeter being MIT lets you ship the *software*; separating and releasing stems of copyrighted songs is a separate rights question for your users.
- **Weight licenses shift.** The Mel-Band RoFormer "Kim" checkpoint has changed license at least twice (unlicensed → GPL-3.0 in June 2025 → MIT tag) and mirrors disagree — re-verify the exact HuggingFace commit/LICENSE before any release, and prefer owning your own weights.
- **CC BY-NC-SA = non-commercial.** BSRNN and FRCRN carry NC signals on their papers/assets; do not embed without explicit commercial licensing.
- **Speech models on singing.** Every speech-tuned model (MossFormer2 SE, DeepFilterNet, VoiceFixer, FRCRN, Miipher) embeds VAD/pause/formant assumptions that can damage sung vocals (flatten vibrato — which can reach ~7 Hz in pop/rock and be mistaken for jitter — smear/shift formants, gate breaths, musical noise on sustained vowels). Always default conservative, protect harmonics/formants, and A/B.
- **Generative ≠ faithful.** Vocoder/diffusion restoration (VoiceFixer, AudioSR, Miipher-class) resynthesizes and can alter the very timbre that defines a singer; reserve for an opt-in tier with A/B.
- **No singing-native denoiser exists off-the-shelf.** The literature has one recent *pitch-shift* restoration paper for singing (arXiv 2601.10345) but no peer-reviewed general singing *denoiser* preserving vibrato/formants — a genuine gap and your opportunity, but it means you can't just license a finished solution.
- **ONNX export gotchas.** STFT/iSTFT and some custom ops export imperfectly; plan to do transforms in Rust and validate numerically (as the Mixxx Demucs→ONNX project had to).
- **Version note:** iZotope RX is now at RX 12 (RX 11 was the prior generation referenced in much of the 2024 coverage); module behavior described is consistent across 11/12 but verify current module availability per edition.
- **Source-quality note:** A few competitor-feature details (CEDAR StageVox singer-focus, exact iZotope/Sonarworks artifact wording, and the viperx/FRCRN license states) come from secondary or shifting sources and were flagged above; confirm against the vendors' own current documentation before relying on them contractually.
